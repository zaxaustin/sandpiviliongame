# How to walk this course

This is both a historical reconstruction and a practical method.
Walk the modules in order. Do the practices on paper or in a plain text file before looking at any solutions or further commentary. The discomfort of the blank page is part of the training.

When you reach Module 4, choose a domain you actually care about (a skill, a craft, a body of knowledge, a practice). The course is only complete when you have produced a real Theoretical Minimum for that domain and a mini-course outline that another person could begin walking.

You may draft the later modules with a cloud model. The Sand Pavilion is the place that hosts and lives the finished work; the strong chat window is currently the best place to do the heavy drafting. Keep the portable Markdown format so the result can drop cleanly into the Pavilion.

## Module 1 — The Historical Gate

**Objective:** Understand what Landau's Theoretical Minimum actually was, why it was named "minimum," and why only ~43 people passed it between 1933/34 and 1961.

**Body:**

Lev Landau designed an entrance examination for anyone who wanted to work seriously in theoretical physics under him. He called it the Theoretical Minimum. The name was deliberate. He was not interested in a theoretical maximum — an endless accumulation of facts and papers. He wanted the load-bearing core: the material without which serious original work is impossible.

The program consisted of roughly nine to eleven oral examinations covering:

- Mathematics I (indefinite integration, ordinary differential equations, vector algebra, tensor analysis)
- Mechanics (Landau–Lifshitz Vol. 1, with specific sections excluded)
- Classical Field Theory (Vol. 2)
- Mathematics II (complex variables, residues, asymptotics, special functions)
- Quantum Mechanics (Vol. 3)
- Quantum Electrodynamics / Relativistic Quantum Theory (Vol. 4)
- Statistical Physics I (Vol. 5)
- Continuum Mechanics (Fluid Mechanics Vol. 6 + Theory of Elasticity Vol. 7)
- Electrodynamics of Continuous Media (Vol. 8)
- Statistical Physics II (selected sections of Vol. 9)

Candidates sat with blank paper. Landau (or later his associates) would give problems. Periodic checks consisted of a soft "Hmm." The examiner was less interested in whether the final number matched a known answer and more interested in whether the candidate could derive and rebuild the argument from first principles. Problems were often reused; the point was the approach, not the memorized solution. After the technical exams came public defense: the candidate had to present recent papers as if they had written them themselves, explaining derivations and experimental context without hiding behind "the author claims."

Only about 43 people ever passed the full set. Seven of them made contributions that changed physics. The exam was not a ranking system or a degree. It was a gate that preserved a coherent level of competence.

The historical value for us is not that every resident must master all ten volumes of Landau–Lifshitz. The value is the design of the gate itself: a clear theoretical minimum, enforced by derivation under observation, process over answer, and public accountability.

**Practice:**
- Write, without looking anything up, a one-paragraph answer to: "Why did Landau call it the Theoretical Minimum instead of the Theoretical Maximum?"
- List three ways this differs from a typical university final exam or online quiz.

**Reflection:**
- What feels different about being asked to reconstruct rather than recall?
- Where in your own learning have you experienced (or avoided) the blank-page test?

**Artifact:** A short handwritten or plain-text note titled "Why Minimum, Not Maximum" + your three differences.

**Reading (optional):**
- Any reliable summary of the Landau Theoretical Minimum syllabus (Stack Exchange reconstructions and Landau Institute pages are useful starting points).
- The five principles as articulated in the source video analysis.

## Module 2 — The Five Principles

**Objective:** Internalize the five operating principles that turned the Theoretical Minimum from a hard test into a training system capable of producing independent thinkers.

**Body:**

The video analysis extracts five principles that operated inside Landau's system. They are not physics-specific. They are a method for building deep competence in any domain that has a real theoretical or practical core.

1. **Define your theoretical minimum.**
   Identify the load-bearing material — the small set of principles, derivations, techniques, or practices without which serious work collapses. Everything else is secondary until this core is owned.

2. **Incorporate it into your identity.**
   You do not "know" the material until you can derive it, rewrite it in your own words, and use its language as your native language. Notes at the door. Blank paper. The knowledge must become part of how you see.

3. **Process, not answer.**
   The examiner cares more about how you approach the problem than whether you arrive at a previously published number. An unexpected but sound path is accepted; a memorized path that collapses under a small variation is not.

4. **Embrace the discomfort.**
   The struggle of derivation, the frustration of the blank page, the temporary inability to proceed — these are not signs of failure. They are the training. Avoiding them produces brittle knowledge.

5. **Defend your answers in public.**
   Explain the material as if you wrote the paper or invented the method. No hiding behind citations or "the author says." Public defense exposes gaps that private study can hide.

These five principles turn a body of knowledge into a living competence. They also give us a template for designing courses that actually produce ability rather than the appearance of coverage.

**Practice:**
- For each of the five principles, write one concrete example from a non-physics domain (cooking, language learning, software design, meditation, woodworking, etc.).
- Choose one principle you personally resist most. Write three sentences explaining why.

**Reflection:**
- Which principle feels most foreign to modern educational habits?
- Where have you seen "process over answer" produce better results than answer-chasing?

**Artifact:** A one-page map: the five principles + your non-physics examples + the principle you resist.

## Module 3 — Modern Translation for Any Domain

**Objective:** Translate the Theoretical Minimum method out of theoretical physics and into a general tool that can be applied to skills, crafts, and bodies of knowledge inside the Sand Pavilion.

**Body:**

The historical exam is extreme because the domain is extreme. The method is portable.

A modern Theoretical Minimum for any domain answers three questions:

1. What is the absolute core that must be derived or performed from first principles, not merely recalled?
2. What does a blank-page test look like in this domain?
3. What does public defense look like?

Examples of domains and possible minima (illustrative only):

- **Conversational language:** Core grammar patterns that generate rather than memorize; ability to reconstruct a tense or construction on the spot; ability to explain a usage error to another learner.
- **Meditation / contemplative practice:** The precise sequence of attention instructions; ability to diagnose and correct common deviations; ability to teach the first ten minutes cleanly.
- **Practical electronics:** Kirchhoff's laws + Thevenin/Norton as living tools; ability to redesign a simple circuit under a constraint change; ability to explain a failure mode without looking it up.
- **Course design itself:** The 7-step protocol; ability to take a skill and produce a coherent ordered set of modules with body text, practice, reflection, and artifact; ability to defend why a given step belongs.

The test is never "have you read all the books." It is "can you rebuild the load-bearing structure on a blank page and then transmit it?"

This is the exact skill the Sand Pavilion needs more of: people who can leave courses that another person can actually walk without the author present.

**Practice:**
- Pick one domain you know moderately well. Write a candidate list of 5–8 items that might constitute its theoretical minimum. Be ruthless — cut anything that is not load-bearing.
- Design one blank-page problem or practice for that domain.

**Reflection:**
- How does the list change when you force yourself to cut it in half?
- What would "public defense" look like for this domain inside the Pavilion (Tutor conversation, bequest note, live walk-through with another resident, etc.)?

**Artifact:** A short "Candidate Theoretical Minimum" document for your chosen domain + one blank-page problem.

## Module 4 — Design Your Own Minimum

**Objective:** Produce a real, usable Theoretical Minimum for one domain you care about, together with a mini-course outline that can later be expanded under the 7-step Course Authoring protocol.

**Body:**

This is the central creative work of the course. You will leave with something concrete.

Choose one domain. It can be:
- A skill you are currently developing
- A craft or practice you already know well enough to teach
- A body of knowledge you want to master more rigorously
- Even "how to design courses for the Sand Pavilion"

Then perform the following steps on paper or in a plain text file (you may later refine with a cloud model):

1. Write the purpose statement in one or two sentences.
2. List the absolute theoretical minimum (aim for 5–12 load-bearing items).
3. For each item, note the form of the blank-page test (derive, reconstruct, perform, diagnose, redesign).
4. Design 3–5 problems or practices that force process rather than recall.
5. Write the public-defense criteria: what must a person be able to explain or demonstrate to another resident?
6. Sketch a 4–7 module mini-course outline that would take a motivated stranger from zero (or from stated prerequisites) to ownership of that minimum. Include a one-sentence objective for each module.

You are not required to write full body text yet. That comes later, either by hand or with a strong model using the portable Markdown format. The goal of this module is the skeleton that has real depth.

**Practice:**
- Complete the six steps above for your chosen domain.
- Time-box the first pass to 60–90 minutes of focused work. Do not perfect; produce.

**Reflection:**
- What was hardest to cut from the minimum list?
- Does the mini-course outline feel like it would produce ability or only the feeling of coverage?

**Artifact:** Your completed "Personal Theoretical Minimum + Mini-Course Outline" document. This is the primary deliverable of the whole course.

## Module 5 — Transmission & Pavilion Integration

**Objective:** Polish the work so it can live inside the Sand Pavilion and, if desired, travel as a packet or bequest. Understand the division of labor between cloud drafting and local hosting.

**Body:**

The Sand Pavilion is an excellent place to host finished courses, to walk them step by step, to link artifacts, to mark completion, and eventually to leave them for others. It is not currently the highest-leverage environment for the heavy creative drafting of rich explanatory text and carefully ordered modules. That work is still best done in a long conversation with a strong cloud model (Claude, ChatGPT, Grok, etc.) that can hold context, iterate, and produce clean Markdown.

The system we want is therefore:

1. **Foundational framework lives in the Pavilion** (this course, the 7-step protocol, the portable Markdown format, Independent Course Graduation requirements).
2. **Heavy drafting happens outside** with any capable model, guided by the protocol and format.
3. **Import and living use happen inside** the Pavilion: the course becomes a real log that can be walked, reflected upon, graduated, and transmitted.

Your work from Module 4 is already close to the portable format. The remaining polish is:

- Clean title, purpose, audience, outcome.
- Make every step understandable in isolation.
- Add a short "How to walk this course" note if needed.
- Check that practice, reflection, and artifact are explicit.
- Decide license and attribution.
- Walk the outline once yourself (or have another person attempt the first module) and note friction points.

When the outline is solid, you can expand any module into full body text — either by hand or by feeding the skeleton to a model with the instruction: "Expand this into the portable Sand Pavilion course format with real explanatory Body text, concrete Practice, Reflection prompts, and a clear Artifact."

The result can then be imported, lived with, and eventually left for others.

**Practice:**
- Take your Module 4 artifact and perform the polish checklist above.
- Write a short note (5–10 sentences) on how this course itself could be improved or extended by a future resident.

**Reflection:**
- What part of the work felt most valuable when done alone on a blank page versus when done in dialogue with a model?
- How does having a clear theoretical minimum change the way you look at other courses you have taken or might create?

**Artifact:** The polished Personal Theoretical Minimum + Mini-Course Outline, ready for expansion or import, plus your short improvement note.
