WHERE THE DAY STARTED

Slice 1 had shipped the night before: a course drafted around the books
you own, received, given a derived syllabus, walked one module at a
time, read aloud. The steward's meditation course was live in his own
save, and he said the only thing left was small front-end work.

Grok's note agreed and put it well — "no new systems, just make what
already works feel smooth" — and then the steward added four things on
reading the plan for slice 2:

  · a way to save your work and look back on it
  · a course FOLDER people can access in the back end
  · progress acknowledged as a badge, not a mission
  · the local AI generating more coursework and practice problems
    ("for meditation it can ask us about karma, list a book about
     karma, and have a book report about what it says")

and then, mid-turn:

  "lets priortize no new systmens but if theres no pathway go make one,
   plz check whats already there first and dont forget screenshots"

That instruction shaped the whole day. The search it demanded found
ELEVEN of the pieces already built. Only two things had no pathway at
all — a folder per course on disk, and a place to write an attempt —
and those are the two that got built. Everything else is a caller.

PASS 0 — AND THE PICTURE FOUND SOMETHING BETTER THAN SPACING
----------------------------------------------------------------

Wrote test/live/course-calm.mjs, which says out loud that it proves
nothing by itself: density is a judgement and only a picture settles it.
What it does is stand the Board and an open course up in a REALISTIC
state — the real 17 KB Junior EE course plus three checklists, which is
what a board looks like after a month rather than on day one — and shoot
them the same way twice.

The numbers it can carry: 14 controls above the first card. Seven of
them category chips on two wrapped rows, TWO OF THEM READING ZERO. A
filter you cannot want to press, drawn above the thing you came to read.

Then I read the second screenshot and found the real thing.

  THE PURPOSE PARAGRAPH WAS ON SCREEN TWICE. Four lines of `.meta` under
  the course title, and the syllabus box directly beneath it opening
  with the identical four lines. Two hundred words of duplicate prose
  above "0 of 9 modules", with Module 1 starting at y=526.

Neither surface was wrong on its own. The syllabus is where "what is
this course" belongs; the meta line predates it by exactly one day. This
is what rule 2 is for — no amount of reading the code says "that
paragraph is on screen twice", and the picture says it instantly.

Fixed as a DERIVATION rather than a deletion: `syllabusHere` decides
once whether the syllabus will speak, and the meta line takes `why` only
when it will not. A checklist keeps its `why` where it always was.

Also found: the syllabus rendered on a three-line checklist as "The
syllabus · 2 modules · no length stated" — a heading promising a
syllabus over a course that has none, which is worse than its absence.
It is for full courses only now, on the same test the badge uses.

Result: 7 controls, not 14. Panel 756px → 632px. Module 1 at y=470.

----------------------------------------------------------------
THE LEARNING DESK
----------------------------------------------------------------

A module has said **Practice:** since the format was written and NOTHING
IN THE PAVILION READ THAT LABEL. course-format.js said so in its own
header — "a field nobody reads is a field that rots… they can be
promoted the moment something actually consumes one." moduleParts() was
written yesterday for the syllabus, which only COUNTED them.

Built as a station in the Study at (5,3), beside the Writing Desk, on
the steward's own line: "for the study table that means study for the
writing table that means wright."

ui/learning-desk.js lives outside overlays.js — the study-table.js
pattern, deps injected once, imports nothing backwards. His words when
he asked for the desk were "i know its gona kill the overlays."

THE ORDER IS THE WHOLE DESIGN:

  1. the module's practice / artifact / reflection, read out of the
     module, not lifted from it
  2. THE BLANK PAGE. You write before anything else exists.
  3. only then the three beats: another problem · explain the theory ·
     look at what I wrote
  4. print it, because the goal is pen and paper away from the screen

The AI buttons are ABSENT, not disabled. A greyed button still tells you
help exists and invites you to want it, which is the thing this desk is
built to postpone. And learnAsk() refuses an empty attempt AT THE DOOR
as well, because a button that is not drawn is still callable from a
console, a stale panel, or a test.

THE HONOR SYSTEM. Asked him directly how the standard should be held:

  "i mainly want to hold this standard by an honor system where people
   are honest about where they come from… if they tell us that they're
   confused or don't understand something then we put them back to the
   beginner course… this isn't just for them this is also for the people
   coming after them… doing it honestly today saves them from a hassle
   tomorrow and the next person from going through the same thing."

So: three words, never a number. not yet · with my notes · on my own,
asked before and again after, optional, in words beside the attempt. A
number is a score, a score gets averaged, and an average of how capable
you felt is a judgement of a person — the stick rule 9 keeps out.

"not yet" is a DOOR, not a shrug: the previous module, this module's
book, the course's prerequisites. It offers. It moves nothing and
un-ticks nothing, and the suite asserts both.

And the `stuck` line, which is the part that is really about other
people. Private, sent nowhere, no path to publish built. What is built
is the field, the honest label ("kept for you — and one day, if you
choose, for whoever walks this next"), and a paragraph in visibility.js
saying where it is meant to go. A promise in a data shape costs nothing;
a promise in a feature nobody asked for costs everything.

THE STUDY TABLE MOVED IN as the second tab. Its 761 lines are untouched;
only where it draws changed, and that is an injected mount id now rather
than a second hard-coded one. Its two live suites followed the room —
same clicks, same assertions, one different door.


----------------------------------------------------------------
TWO BUGS THAT ONLY LOOKING FOUND
----------------------------------------------------------------

1 · moduleParts() TRUNCATED EVERY MULTI-LINE PART TO ITS FIRST LINE.

The `m` flag made `$` in the lookahead mean END OF LINE, so a lazy match
stopped at the first newline. The real Junior EE module asks three
things and the screen showed one. Two-thirds of what the author wrote,
gone, with nothing on screen to say so.

Invisible to every test until the desk rendered it, and invisible for
exactly the reason rule 1 names: the guard fed it "**Practice:** do it."
— a tidy one-liner — and the syllabus only ever counted parts rather
than rendering them. The guard reads courses/*.course.md now and counts
bullets. Broken on purpose: it catches.

2 · PRESSING AN HONESTY CHIP THREW AWAY THE STUCK LINE.

Every press on the panel redraws it, and a redraw is an empty form. The
attempt saved fine, so nothing looked wrong — the sentence written for
whoever comes next was simply gone. The Receive panel had this exact bug
a day earlier ("pressing a door threw away the baseline"), and it is the
same fix: the panel does not own the words, a module-level draft does.
The live suite now presses a chip AFTER typing, on purpose.

And a third, from the same screenshot: mdLite() alone rendered a
three-bullet practice as one running sentence. courseProse() lives in
ui/dom.js now, beside the escaping it depends on, and both callers share
it. A second copy of an ESCAPER is the worst thing to have two of.


----------------------------------------------------------------
LOOKING BACK, AND THE FOLDER
----------------------------------------------------------------

"i wanna see a way where people can save their work and look back on it
 · i also wanna see where we're gonna hold all these files together and
 if we can combine them all into a course folder that people can access
 in the back end"

CHECKED FIRST, AND IT CHANGED THE ANSWER. gatherRecords() in
data/records.js is already the Pavilion's one walk over the save —
milestone, reading, day, packet. So looking back is a FIFTH KIND, not a
new room: `work` joins them, an attempt shows up in the Records Hall
beside the days you finished, and the desk's "your work on this course"
view is that same walk filtered by the course id it now carries in
`ref`. A second timeline would be a second thing to keep in step.

THE FOLDER IS THE ONE GENUINELY NEW PATHWAY. libraryWrite puts flat
.txt files in ONE directory for book text; nothing in this app made a
DIRECTORY and nothing opened one.

  userData/courses/<slug>/
    README.txt   what this folder is, in plain words
    course.md    the course itself, portable, re-importable
    syllabus.md  derived, rewritten on every save
    work/        your attempts, one file per module
    handouts/    anything printed

data/course-folder.js is PURE — returns { sub, name, content }, touches
no disk and no window — so npm test can assert the whole file set and
the desktop and browser paths share one answer.

NOTHING IN IT WRITES MARKDOWN. course.md is lessonFromCourse() →
lessonToMarkdown(), and the Electron suite asserts it is byte-identical
to what the export button produces AND that it parses straight back in.
A folder whose course.md the Pavilion cannot read is a folder of
souvenirs, not a course you can hand to somebody.

test/live/course-folder.cjs is Electron and that is not optional: a
browser has no bridge and could only stub one. It reads the files back
with fs, OUTSIDE the app, because a bridge that returns {ok:true} and
writes nothing is this project's oldest bug shape. Five path-traversal
shapes tried, five refused. And it refuses to run at all if it cannot
reach main.cjs's handlers, rather than reporting that as a red result
about the code.

The folder write rides the keep-attempt press, but only if a folder
already exists — writing the first one is a decision, not a side effect.
On the web build it is ABSENT and says so, falling back to the
saveFile/<a download> path already written six times here.


----------------------------------------------------------------
BADGES, AND ONE MORE MODULE
----------------------------------------------------------------

"some way of acknowledging progress not as a mission but as like a badge
 system to support them"

data/badges.js ALREADY IS that system — its own header says "things to
try without a forced walkthrough". So five rows, not a second progress
system beside it. Every one fires from a button; badges.js already
records why, from the first-plan scar: "a badge toast firing while
someone is mid-sentence is a machine congratulating them for typing."

first-artifact is deliberately narrow — the module has to have ASKED you
to make something and you have to have written an attempt at it.

buildExtendPrompt() for the fourth ask. His karma example IS the
format's own shape: the question is the Objective, the book is Reading +
Why this text, the report is the Artifact. Nothing new to invent.

Deterministic first: it is handed the modules that exist, which of YOUR
books touch the subject (the same shelfMatches the Board uses), and your
own stuck lines. That last one is why extending here beats extending in
a chat window — no general model knows the sticking point was voltage
versus current and the water analogy was what moved it.

It lands as a DRAFT. If a model returns the whole course instead of one
module the last heading is taken and it SAYS so, rather than quietly
doubling the course. Works with no model at all via copy-the-prompt,
which is the half that matters on a laptop.

One real bug from reading the failure rather than the assertion:
parseCourse() requires a title — reasonably — and a bare `## Module 10`
has none, so a perfectly good reply parsed to zero modules. The fragment
gets a title it does not keep, rather than teaching the format an
exception.


----------------------------------------------------------------
THREE GUARDS WERE READING LESS THAN THEY CLAIMED
----------------------------------------------------------------

All three found by ADDING to what they watch, which is the only way this
kind of hole ever surfaces.

  · The window-export scan missed the FIRST name on every multi-line
    entry — `^` with no m flag meant start-of-string, so the alternation
    only fired after a `{` or a `,`. openLearningDesk was in the block
    and the opener check reported it missing. A false alarm is the kind
    half of that bug; the same hole can believe a name present when it
    is not.

  · The badge award scan read a HAND-KEPT LIST OF SEVEN FILES and there
    are eight. ui/learning-desk.js landed with three award sites in it
    and the guard said three badges were awarded by nothing at all. It
    walks every .js under src/game now, and fails loudly below fifteen.

  · KNOWN_STATION_KINDS was a hand-list whose own comment said it "must
    stay in sync by hand with the st.kind=== checks in main.js — there
    is no way to check that automatically without parsing the file."
    That is rule 4's shape, admitted in the comment, inside the guard
    whose job is catching drift. Parsing the file was all it took.

AND ONE GUARD WAS BORN DEAD, caught by breaking it: it allowed 300 chars
between `function syllabusOwned(step){` and `shelfMatches(`, and the
very next function in the file calls shelfMatches() on its second line.
Deleting the real call left the suite GREEN against a different
function. Brace-bounded now, and it names step.reading.

test/sabotage/ is new. Four runners, kept because the evidence is the
point — every one found a dead guard or a dead runner on its first run.
Two rules in its README: verify a green baseline before breaking
anything (2026-08-14's runner reported "all 11 caught" while smoke.mjs
had a syntax error), and match either line ending (a literal multi-line
search found nothing against the CRLF working copy and reported "the
line to break is not there" — the same shape as the CRLF scrape of
2026-08-13).
