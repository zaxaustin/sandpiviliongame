This landed on prepared ground in a way that doesn't happen often.
RESEARCH-ROOM-PLAN.md (written 2026-07-12) already said, in as many words,
that the way to find out what the research room actually needs is to use it
on a real subject, and it already named electronics. What it never had was a
PROJECT. A universal remote is a better one than I would have chosen, and
the reasons are worth writing down because they're the reasons it works as
a test of the Hall rather than just a fun build:

  - It is checkable. The TV changes channel or it doesn't. The Hall's four
    seed investigations are ALL soft-science claims where being wrong is
    arguable, and that has been quietly flattering the room. Electronics is
    nothing but falsifiable.
  - It has a real idea at its heart. Remotes don't send plain IR — they
    chop the beam at ~38 kHz so the receiver can band-pass everything else
    away. That's AM and noise rejection, the spine of radio, met on a
    breadboard for eight dollars.
  - Its primary literature is genuinely free AND legitimately shelvable:
    datasheets and manufacturer app notes are published free, and the two
    best textbooks (Kuphaldt's "Lessons In Electric Circuits", Design
    Science License; NEETS, US Navy, public domain) can go on the Science
    shelf without bending provenance-at-the-door at all. That is unusual.
  - It ends in an object. The printer in the room prints the case.

WHAT WAS BUILT — THE BENCH (gap #1)
-----------------------------------
A build had no home. This took a minute to see clearly, because the Hall
LOOKS like it has one: self-experiments. But a self-experiment is shaped
`a practice + one 1-5 measure, logged daily`. That is exactly right for
meditation and exactly wrong for making a thing. Building is:

    what I expected  ->  what I did  ->  what actually happened

and the ORDER is the whole point, not a formatting preference.

So the Bench is deliberately TWO-PHASE. You save a prediction on its own,
and only then does the "what happened" half become reachable. There is no
path anywhere in the UI to edit a guess after you know the answer, because
by then it's already written down. That single constraint is this room's
creed made structural — the same trick the investigation form plays with
its required falsifier field, and the same reason Stage 2 of the remote
project decodes a frame by hand before letting a library do it.

This is SCIENCE-RESEARCH-HALL-PLAN.md's "L0 - the print log", generalised
past 3D printing on the way in: a circuit, a print, a repair and a recipe
all log identically, and the first occupant turned out to be a remote.

  - data.hall.builds, migrated for old saves in entities.js
  - four outcomes (as-predicted / surprised / failed / inconclusive), the
    same badge treatment as VERDICTS
  - the Investigator reads a log under a new buildContext branch, pointed
    at the one thing he's uniquely good for: the pattern ACROSS steps that
    you are too close to see — the same wrong assumption showing up three
    times, a quantity you keep guessing high, a step where you stopped
    measuring and started hoping.

Three things, and the middle one was the interesting one.

1. DATASHEETS — a `kind` on a shelf entry
------------------------------------------
A datasheet is genuinely not a book and not a paper. You never read one
start to finish. It belongs to a PART rather than an author. You come back
to it fifty times for one number. So it does not get a browse; it gets a
LOOKUP. At the Computer:

    ds              list them by part number
    ds tsop         open TSOP38238 immediately

`ls` grew a three-character kind column (ds / pap / own / blank), because a
terminal should tell you what a thing IS without you opening it.

2. BOOKS YOU OWN ON PAPER — the one worth thinking about
---------------------------------------------------------
The steward owns Practical Electronics for Inventors as a physical book.
Can't scan it, and shouldn't — it's in copyright and this project doesn't
launder provenance.

The tempting answer is "so it can't be part of the Pavilion." That's wrong,
and seeing why took a minute. A `physical` entry has NO TEXT and never
will — that's the honest part, stated on the card. What it can still do:

  - stay findable in `ls`, `find`, `owned`
  - hold page-referenced notes
  - be cited as evidence in an investigation or a bench step
  - and the load-bearing one: THE RESIDENTS ARE TOLD YOU OWN IT

That last one is the whole feature. referenceShelfBlock() feeds the
Computer, the Tutor and the Investigator a list of what's on the shelf
across the room, with an explicit instruction: when one of these genuinely
covers the question, SAY SO and send them to it by name — never pretend to
quote it, never invent a page number. So the answer becomes "you own
Scherz & Monk, chapter 4 covers exactly this, go and look" instead of a
thinner explanation from memory.

That is a real bookshelf becoming part of the platform with no scanner and
no copyright problem, and it is a better answer than scanning would have
been. Pointing is the service.

3. THE ROOM — and it cost nothing
----------------------------------
"if we dont need another room thats fine but it would be nice to have
somthing like a science lab."

Went looking for where to put it and found the Pavilion had already
promised it. The Workshop's Unfinished Floor has carried a MAKER'S BENCH
placeholder since that floor was built, and its sign reads, verbatim:

    "A home for real-world physical projects — a build, a repair, a
     garden. Distinct from the Grant Desk's funding focus and the
     Research Desk's thinking-through focus. Not open yet."

That is exactly what got built this week. So the placeholder OPENED rather
than a new room appearing beside it. A promise kept beats a promise added,
and that floor is one room less unfinished. Sprite key stays `makersbench`
(it was always drawn as a workbench); only the visible name changed.

Worth noting this quietly overrules the Hall plan's basement instinct. The
REASONING there survives — a lab is where you're allowed to break things,
and that belongs apart from where claims get appraised carefully — it just
turned out to be one floor up instead of one down.

What the Lab actually IS: the gathering view. RESEARCH-ROOM-PLAN.md Phase 1
asked for a "My Research" home and said to build it from what real use
showed was needed rather than guessing in advance. Real use had now shown:
builds, datasheets, the paper shelf, investigations, experiments. So it is
that, wearing a lab coat. It OWNS NOTHING — every count is read live from
where the thing already lives and every button walks you there, so there's
no second copy to drift out of sync. The one thing it surfaces on its own
is predictions still waiting on a result, because that's the only half of a
bench entry that goes stale.
