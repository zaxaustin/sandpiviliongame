> Aristotle’s account of what a good life actually consists of — not a rulebook, but a case for virtue as a trained habit.

## What it is

Ten books arguing that virtue isn’t a feeling or a rule but a disposition built through practice, typically found as a mean between two opposing failures — courage between recklessness and cowardice, and so on.

## How to read it

Slower than it looks. The individual claims are plain, but Aristotle’s method is to test each one from several angles before trusting it — worth matching his pace rather than skimming for conclusions.
