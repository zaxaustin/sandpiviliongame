> A dialogue on the eve of battle: Krishna counsels Arjuna on duty, action without attachment, and the nature of the self — the heart of the Mahabharata, and one of the most beloved teachings in the world.

## What it is

Seven hundred verses across eighteen chapters, set on the field of Kurukshetra. The warrior Arjuna, unwilling to fight his own kin, lays down his bow; Krishna’s reply to his despair becomes a complete map of the spiritual life.

## The central teaching

Act because the act is right, not for its reward — action without attachment to its fruit. Around this turn three paths to the same freedom: devotion (bhakti), knowledge (jnana), and disciplined action (karma).

## How to read it

Slowly, a chapter at a sitting. Arnold’s Victorian verse is stately and musical; where it feels ornate, read for the argument beneath the music. Many keep the second chapter especially close.
