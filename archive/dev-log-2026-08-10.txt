`text_key` only ever meant "the MinIO object". There was no column for

  Don Quixote · The Divine Comedy · The Complete Works of Shakespeare

Their text was in 59 real .txt files under userData/library, written by
shelveAsPersonal()'s fallback whenever MinIO was not answering. THE ONLY
RECORD OF WHICH FILE BELONGED TO WHICH BOOK LIVED IN localStorage. Lose
the save and those 59 become unopenable with the files untouched on
disk.

  books.local_file           the filename, nullable, alongside text_key
  v_book_homes               the honest three-way answer in SQL, matching
                             storageOf() in data/book-storage.js
  books_has_text             an index on "can I actually open this"

TWO NULLABLE COLUMNS, NOT A SCHEME INSIDE ONE. A book can legitimately
be in BOTH homes - written to MinIO and cached locally, or moved between
them - and squeezing two into one column forces a parse and loses the
ability to say "both". upsertCard COALESCEs each independently, so a
save that knows only one pointer can never erase the other. That is the
same shape as the shelf-assignment merge, and one of the seven sabotage
cases.

  BEFORE   326 docker · 89 with no pointer at all
  AFTER    326 docker · 59 this machine · 30 genuinely summary-only

The 30 are real: books owned on paper, cards with no text yet. They are
supposed to look like that, and now they are the ONLY things that do.

Backfilled deterministically from the filenames (they match the slugs
exactly, 59 of 59), and going forward hydrateFromDb carries the pointer
up on its own. Verified with an EMPTY save against the real container:
the shelf line reads "🐳 326 Docker · 💾 59 this machine · 📝 30 summary
only · local shelf 59/100" with nothing in localStorage at all - which
is precisely the thing that was impossible yesterday.

AND THE MIGRATION BROKE EVERY WRITE FOR TEN MINUTES. upsertCard went
from 10 placeholders to 11; one caller in store.js and four in
docker-home.cjs still passed 10. Postgres refuses the WHOLE prepared
statement on a parameter-count mismatch, so chapter syncing stopped

Rule 4 in its purest form: a count in one file that must match a count

A BULK DROP NEVER REACHED THE DATABASE
================================================================

  "would adding new books update the backend data for them?"
  "the multi load did not but one at a time did"

Exactly right, reported from real use, and the cause was one line.

The ONLY call to upsertCard from the app lived inside chapterInfo(),
which runs when a book's full text is PAGINATED - that is, when you OPEN
it. So:

  one at a time   you drop it, you read it, chapterInfo fires, the row
                  lands in the catalogue                          ✓
  forty at once   you drop forty and open none of them, and NOT ONE of
                  them exists to the database                      ✗

Every consequence is invisible until you go looking. A resident's
catalogue lookup could not find them (searchBooks reads the books table),
a note could not point at one (notes.slug is a foreign key into it), and
they were missing from the Records Hall. The books were fine the whole
time; the index simply did not know they existed. This is the same shape
as the category bug earlier today - the data was there and one join was
not - and it is why "is it in the save" and "is it in the catalogue" are
two different questions that must both be asked.

shelveAsPersonal() now writes the card at IMPORT, and all four import
paths funnel through it (drop-anywhere, bulk drop, the review queue, the
by-hand form). Fire-and-forget like syncChapters: nothing waits on it, a
browser falls straight through, and a failure cannot cost you the book,
which is already shelved and persisted two lines above.

AN UNFILED BOOK GOES UP AS NULL, not 'Personal'. `shelf IS NULL` is what
the sorter's work queue asks for, and writing 'Personal' would quietly
empty the pile of books waiting to be sorted - the tool would report
nothing to do while the pile sat there.

THE PAGE CACHE IS REQUIRED, NOT A NICETY
------------------------------------------
Paginating a 563 KB book and tokenising it for BM25 on every message is
real repeated CPU on a machine whose actual limit is COOLING. pagesOf()
in retrieval.js: four slots, keyed on slug + stamp + text length,
oldest-used evicted.

slot would THRASH between two carried texts - worse than no cache,
since it pays the eviction as well as the work. Measured: a warm second
ask, different question, rebuilt the Monk's whole prompt in ~10 ms.

caches UNITS and detected chapters, not just pages.

which is not a rate. It is a BUDGET: every reply took the same ~1.8
seconds, so a 100-character answer arrived at ~50 cps and a
2,000-character one at ~1,100 cps. THE MORE THE RESIDENT SAID, THE
LESS TIME YOU GOT PER WORD. Exactly inverted, and it governed every
non-streaming reveal in the game.

It also gave one character two voices. The Monk's real `pace: 34`
applied on the STREAMING path only; pocket the conversation and the
same 900-character reply came back through the budget at ~500 cps.
26 seconds if you watched, 1.8 if you looked away. A speaking rate
that depends on whether anyone is looking is not a speaking rate.

Fixed: DEFAULT_PACE (90 cps, ~290 wpm) exported from roles.js,
paceOf() falls back to it so EVERY resident resolves to a positive
rate, and typewriteChatText() takes cps. One reveal path.


THE REVEAL IS A CLOCK, NOT AN ACCUMULATOR
----------------------------------------------------------------
First version added `round(cps*TICK/1000)` characters per tick. At 34
cps and a 50 ms tick that rounds 1.7 UP TO 2, and the Monk came out at
40 cps - an 18% lie in the one number that is supposed to be his
character. Caught by measuring it, not by reading it.

Both reveals now compute the head from Date.now() against a start
stamp. Measured after: 90.0 and 34.0, exactly the declared numbers.
It also means a tick the browser was too busy to run costs nothing.


THREE BUGS FOUND BY RUNNING IT, NONE BY READING IT
----------------------------------------------------------------
1. THE PACE TIMER OUTLIVED ITS CONVERSATION. paceTimer is
   module-level and was only ever stopped by d.streaming going null at
   the end of a turn. Walk out mid-reply and it kept ticking - then
   `if(paceTimer) return` in startPacedReveal BLOCKED the next
   conversation's reveal while the orphan wrote the old reply's slice
   into the new bubble. The counter went BACKWARDS, 190 chars to 30.
   Now: closeDialog() and openChatDialog() stop it, and the tick
   checks state.dialog===d, because two ways to leave a conversation
   is two ways to leak.

2. TICK was still the setInterval delay after its `const` was
   removed. "TICK is not defined", three times, on the one path that
   runs it - CLAUDE.md's own gotcha, caught only because
   page.on('pageerror') was attached.

3. `new URL(...).pathname` on a project path containing a space hands
   back "sand%20pavilion%20game", and mkdirSync cheerfully created a
   directory of that name on the Desktop. Screenshots now go to
   test/live/_pace-*.png like every other suite, which .gitignore
   already covers.


"WE SHOULD BE ABLE TO PAUSE THAT SPEAKING CHAIN"
----------------------------------------------------------------
Mid-session, and it was the right correction:

  "we should be able to pause that speaking chain its for users not
   for testing"

The reveal had exactly one control - click the log, skip to the end -
which is the only control that CANNOT express "wait, I am still
reading that". A pace with a skip and no hold is a pace done TO the
visitor, which is the precise opposite of the point.

So: a hold. One flag on the conversation, both reveal paths resume
from the character it was left on, and the row is only on screen while
something is actually being said (a permanent paused button that does
nothing nine tenths of the time is rule 5 with an icon on it).

It has to survive two things that each quietly undo it, and both were
found by sabotage:
  - the next arriving token, because updateStreamingBubble() calls
    startPacedReveal() on every one of them;
  - the handover from the streaming bubble to the settled one, which
    re-renders from the transcript and would snap to full length.

POCKETED IS NOT PAUSED, either. The counter advances while the chat is
in your pocket; only the DOM write is skipped. Come back after a
minute and you are where they have got to, not at the beginning.


GUARD A, AND WHY IT IS NOT ENOUGH ON ITS OWN
----------------------------------------------------------------
npm test guard A: every ROLE_KEYS entry resolves to a positive rate in
a readable range, no reveal function body contains `.length / <digit>`
(comments stripped first, brace-matched bodies, and it fails if it
read fewer bodies than it meant to), the rate reaches the typewriter,
and the tick does not stop on d.minimized. Six sabotages, all six
caught by the intended check.

But guard A holds the SHAPE of the code. It could not have caught the
quantisation, the orphaned timer, or TICK. So there is now a behaviour
suite as well:

test/live/speaking-pace.mjs - 15 checks against a FAKE OLLAMA on an
ephemeral port (named in the save as the one connection, so it never
fights the real Ollama for 11434 and never needs one installed).
Everything above the endpoint is the shipped path. It measures
characters actually on screen over time:

  - a 1,000-char reply and a 115-char one reveal at the same cps
    (ratio 1.00 - this single assertion IS the original bug)
  - the Monk at his own 34, everyone else at 90
  - the 20 s cap is a safety, measured separately, because measuring
    the Monk on a 1,000-char reply measures the CAP (~50 cps) and not
    his pace - two behaviours measured as one is how a wrong number
    looks right
  - the pocket, the hold, the handover, and one click still ends it

TWO OF ITS OWN CHECKS WERE BORN DEAD and only sabotage found them:
  - the pocket check used the default 200 ms stream, so the reply had
    already LANDED before pocketing and the resume ran through a
    different (already correct) mechanism. Restoring the freeze passed
    clean. It needs a 9 s stream.
  - the hold check resumed BEFORE the handover ever happened, so
    removing `|| d.revealPaused` from the settle call passed clean.

Both now fail on their sabotage. Ten sabotages this part, three born
dead - the running count is eleven born-dead guards, and every one of
them was found the same way.
