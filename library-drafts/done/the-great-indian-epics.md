## Summary

A single-volume retelling of the Ramayana and the Mahabharata — the two epics nearly everything else in Hindu literature and religious life refers back to, the way Homer sits behind so much of the Greek canon already on this Library's Classics shelf. This edition exists specifically to make both approachable in one sitting rather than across their full, immense original length.

## Section 1 — Two epics, one shelf

The Ramayana follows Rama's exile and the recovery of his wife Sita; the Mahabharata (which the Bhagavad-Gita, shelved alongside this, is actually one small episode of) follows the succession war between the Pandavas and Kauravas. Reading them together here is deliberate — most readers meet the Gita first without ever seeing the vast narrative it's actually embedded in.

## Section 2 — Why a retelling, not the full original

The real Sanskrit epics run to tens of thousands of verses each; a retelling trades that scale for an actual first read that finishes. Treat this as the on-ramp, the same role Bulfinch's *Mythology* plays for Greek myth — a real, honest introduction, not a substitute for the full text if it hooks you.
