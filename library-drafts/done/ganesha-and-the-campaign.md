## Summary

A traditional story about why Ganesha is invoked before any undertaking, told here the way a steward actually heard and took it — and the real, present-tense practice it produced: kneeling to Ganesha and asking for safe passage whenever the road ahead starts to feel uneasy, before finding out the hard way whether it needed to be.

## Section 1 — The story

Shiva once set out on a campaign — a real military campaign, armies and marches and all the ordinary logistics of getting somewhere to fight. Before the first battle was ever fought, the journey itself kept going wrong: one obstacle, then another, small delays compounding into real ones, nothing catastrophic, just a road that refused to go smoothly. Shiva stopped and realized what he'd skipped — he had not kneeled to his own son, Ganesha, remover of obstacles, and asked for safe passage before setting out. He did: praised Ganesha's brilliance, honored the bond between them as father and son, and asked plainly for the road ahead to be clear. From that point on, the rest of the campaign went smoothly, start to finish.

## Section 2 — Why this is a practice, not just a story

Told and heard seriously, not as decoration: whenever something in a day or a plan starts to feel unsettled — ill at ease, obstacles compounding for no clear reason — the practice this story produced is to kneel to Ganesha directly, praise his brilliance and his brotherly nature, and ask for safe passage before assuming the difficulty was ever necessary in the first place. Even Shiva needed the reminder. The statue in the Keep, mirrored across from the Buddha shrine, exists so this isn't just a story on a page but something to actually kneel in front of.
