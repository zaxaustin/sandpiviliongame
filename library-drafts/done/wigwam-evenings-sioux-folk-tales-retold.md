# Wigwam Evenings: Sioux Folk Tales Retold

**Tradition:** Native American
**License:** Public Domain (Project Gutenberg)
**Source URL:** https://www.gutenberg.org/ebooks/28099
**Attribution:** Charles A. Eastman (Ohiyesa) and Elaine Goodale Eastman, 1909

## Summary

A collection of actual Sioux (Dakota) oral-tradition stories, retold by Eastman (who grew up hearing them) and his wife Elaine Goodale Eastman, framed as an evening's storytelling around a real fire rather than an anthropological transcript. Companion volume to *The Soul of the Indian* — that book gives the belief system, this one gives the stories that actually carried it.

## Section 1 — Stories as the real teaching method

The book's frame — a grandmother telling stories to children on winter evenings — isn't decoration; it reflects how this material was actually transmitted for generations before it was ever written down. Reading the frame alongside the tales matters as much as the tales themselves.

## Section 2 — Oral tradition, not folklore-as-curiosity

Where much period folklore collection treated Native stories as specimens to catalogue, Eastman's retelling (same standpoint stated directly in *Soul of the Indian*'s preface) keeps the stories inside their own living context — moral instruction and entertainment at once, the way they were actually used, not artifacts behind glass.
