# The Soul of the Indian: An Interpretation

**Tradition:** Native American
**License:** Public Domain (Project Gutenberg)
**Source URL:** https://www.gutenberg.org/ebooks/340
**Attribution:** Charles A. Eastman (Ohiyesa), Santee Dakota, 1911

## Summary

A Dakota physician and writer's own account of traditional Native spirituality — the "Great Mystery," solitary wilderness worship, an unwritten moral code — written explicitly to correct the "dry bones" of outside ethnographic accounts with a genuine insider's voice. Eastman says so directly in his own preface: this is written "from the human, not the ethnological standpoint," against material he calls "modern and hybrid" or invented for sale.

## Section 1 — Worship without a temple

Eastman opens with what he calls the "Great Mystery": traditional worship as silent, solitary, and unmediated — practiced alone in nature rather than through communal ritual or a fixed symbol, closer to what a contemplative tradition might call direct, wordless attention than to organized religion in the Western sense.

## Section 2 — Written against being spoken for

What makes this text worth shelving over a 19th-century missionary or anthropologist's account of the same subject is stated in the preface itself: Eastman is explicitly answering those outside accounts, and the commercial fabrication some reservation visitors were paid to produce, with his own actual upbringing and ancestral teaching. A rare case of the primary, public-domain-era source also being the more honest and less mediated one.
