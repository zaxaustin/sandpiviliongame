## Summary

Captain Joshua Slocum's own account of becoming the first person to sail alone around the world (1895–1898), in a 37-foot sloop he rebuilt himself from a rotting hulk given to him for free. A real, first-person record of self-reliance under genuinely unforgiving conditions — not survival theory, but the actual log of someone who did it.

## Section 1 — Built before it sailed

The book opens not on the ocean but in a pasture, with Slocum rebuilding the *Spray* almost entirely by hand — recaulking, replanking, refitting a boat everyone else considered done. The famous first line of the real voyage only comes after this: the self-reliance the book is remembered for started at the workbench, not the helm.

## Section 2 — A working log, not an adventure story written after the fact

Each chapter reads like an actual logbook stretched into prose — landfalls, weather, running repairs, the specific trouble of a shaking-down cruise from Boston to Gloucester before the real voyage even begins. The value here isn't inspiration; it's the texture of what solo self-sufficiency actually requires day to day, told by someone who kept notes as he lived it.
